    What this model leaves out
    • A boat with its engine off is silent. Its track ends where it was last heard.
    • Sound travels in straight lines through uniform water. Echoes off the seabed and surface are folded into the loss and timing error settings.
    • Arrival timing assumes the processing corrects for the boat moving during each listen. Without that, a 10 kn boat smears arrival times by about 25 ms.
    • Hydrophones on one buoy sit closer than half a wavelength, so they are given no gain over sea noise. Bearings assume each hydrophone's own noise is independent.
    • Boats are told apart by engine pitch. Two boats with the same pitch are not handled.
    • Buoys stay where you put them. Drift on the mooring is part of the timing error setting.
    • Sound takes up to 3 seconds to cross the square. The tracker ignores that delay.
    Where the defaults come from
    • Loss over distance is set so one buoy hears a boat at about 3 km. PNNL-19169 (Matzner and others, 2010) found a small vessel "could reliably be detected from 3–5 km away" with one hydrophone in water 6 to 7 m deep.
    • The same report measured about 126 dB from an outboard boat at 200 m. The default boat here comes to about 121 dB at 200 m, so it is on the quiet side.
    • Sea noise of 70 dB is a moderate sea on the Wenz curves (Wenz, 1962, Journal of the Acoustical Society of America 34). Shallow coastal water often runs higher. Not checked for the trial site.
    • The 16 dB signal threshold comes from this simulator's own audio test. Below about 15 dB the pitch measurement breaks down.
    • Pitch wobble and timing error are estimates with no source. Replace them with numbers from a field recording.
